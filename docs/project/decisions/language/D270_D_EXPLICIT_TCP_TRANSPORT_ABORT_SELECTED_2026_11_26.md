## Selected contract
Ordinary message TcpConnection.abort() -> Future<TcpConnection>. No new grammar, Future state, generic Abortable protocol or authority. The authentic local TCP capability grants the operation; no delegation to TcpAcceptor, Network, Process, Actor or Future, no Actor/P/cross-Process transfer. No implicit abort on termination, cancellation, deadline expiry or borrowed wrapper use.

Invocation irreversibly establishes logical abort cutover before returning a fresh ordinary Future. Open-only operations are rejected thereafter. Abort may escalate a pending ordinary close; it initiates backend retirement independent of peer reading, without voluntary further production. Already in-flight native work may still establish a permitted contiguous output prefix; no rollback, public count, retry or mandatory TCP reset. Preserve previously committed results and terminal Futures. Uncommitted reads fail with zero logical consumption; inaccessible buffered state may be retired. Uncommitted writes fail without contribution; committed incomplete writes fail with their permitted prefix. Existing independently committed errors/results retain precedence. Use IOLifecycleError for cutover-rejected/precommit terminated work and IOError for committed operations whose success cannot be established because of retirement; no new Error prototype. Per-operation errors fresh; same failed Future preserves identity.

Abort is irreversible, not cancelled by its Future cancellation. Repeated abort returns fresh Futures observing one logical retirement, same recorded failure cause in same domain. One physical release. Close after abort observes retirement; abort during close allows committed operation aftermath to settle without further peer progress; close may succeed if required aftermath and release succeed even if earlier writes failed. Abort after successful close succeeds without second release. Failed retirement leaves resource permanently unusable. Exact failed-close/abort and half-close ordering remain to reconcile before ratification.

No successful retirement before required terminal outcomes and safe resource release, with late callbacks prevented from reviving connection or affecting reused descriptor. Residual native obligations remain under producer/runtime custody until safe release; no orphan work. Actor termination does not implicitly abort; explicit already-committed abort continues under runtime custody. Explicit invocation during authorized termination cleanup follows existing authorized dynamic extent; no ordinary code resurrection. Process/Context cleanup preserves resource obligations. Pending connect/listen/accept/resolver acquisitions are not abort targets; existing late acquisition custody unchanged.

TLS never synthesizes close_notify or authenticated EOF from transport termination; truncation rules apply when relevant, committed TLS effects preserved. Borrowed HTTP/TLS wrappers gain no abort authority; ordinary owning close responsibility alone does not grant automatic abort. Explicit authorized HTTP hosting policy may compose D269 observation with D270 retirement and recover quota only after safe retirement. No automatic changes to current wrapper APIs. No preemption, hard real-time claim or guarantee of remote receipt.

## D270-D1-B — Owner-approved cutover refinement (2026-11-26)

The owner explicitly stated “apruebo D270-D1-B.” This selects preservation of successful completion of an effect attempt already admitted before the abort cutover, when that attempt establishes the operation's full successful effect. Pending publication alone cannot turn that success into failure. Preserve independently established failures; incomplete committed writes fail with their permitted contiguous prefix. No new effect attempt may be admitted after cutover, even for an already committed write. Reject D1-A (unconditional failure of every pending write). This refines, not replaces, D270-D.

[Pinned Truffle comparative evidence and exact approval](../../evidence/D270/D270_D1_B_TRUFFLE_PRECEDENTS_AND_OWNER_APPROVAL_2026_11_26.md).
