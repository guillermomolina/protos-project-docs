## GITHUB021 delta and explicit selection

F2 remains the architecture: independent Timing grant, three ordinary messages, no generic Future race, no implicit producer cancellation, no extra Future state, exact Integer coordinates, explicit same-Process spawn-only provisioning, no clock polling on ordinary Future transitions. D253-C1 remains separate and unchanged.

The selected F2.1-B rule addresses pending Timing Futures at Actor termination:

| Alternative | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| A: keep the Actor alive until all Timing publications settle | Adds termination obligations for mere temporal observation, including potentially distant timers; rejected. |
| B: retire local Timing registrations and cancel still-pending Timing Futures | Fits existing non-task Future termination and host discard/custody rules; selected. |
| C: publish directly from provider threads outside the Actor domain | Bypasses the existing execution/publication boundary; rejected. |

B explicitly permits Actor termination to cancel a still-pending temporal observation even when its outcome has already been selected internally but not yet published as terminal. This is a narrow termination exception, not ordinary cancellation semantics. It never rewrites an already-terminal Future or changes the observed source; it adds no termination wait, hidden Task, guest callback on a host thread, or general exception to committed lifecycle-close handling. Retire registrations without revoking other Actors' grants. Preserve Actor termination and Process/Context custody; no resurrection.

Ordinary observer cancellation wins only before the irreversible result decision and before D. Deadline equality favors expiry; delayed notification cannot turn a pre-deadline source publication into a timeout. At admission at or beyond D, expiry wins even for a terminal source; before D an already-terminal source is mirrored. Mirror value and Error identities, or cancelled state, under the existing Future rules. Host revocation in an active Actor preserves a previously decided result; otherwise fail with IOLifecycleError unless expiry can already be established. Provider failure is IOError, semantic argument or foreign-domain source rejection InvalidIOArgument, bounded registration refusal IOCapacityExhausted. Validate semantic arguments before temporal sampling; successfully dispatched asynchronous operations use failed Futures. Negative or non-Integer deadlines are invalid; a valid nonnegative Integer is not rejected merely for exceeding signed long range. DeadlineExceeded directly delegates Error. Exact timer scheduling and failure/cutover arbitration must preserve these choices; no inferred new semantics.

D270/I184 supplies explicit transport abort for an owner dealing with backpressure after committed TCP output. Timing observation, producer cancellation and transport retirement remain distinct, explicit operations. No implicit close/abort, no borrowed authority escalation, no rollback, no TLS integrity weakening, no hard cleanup bound, no automatic hostile-ingress safety claim. This resolves the earlier dependency concern without changing F2.
